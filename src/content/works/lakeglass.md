## A field guide, not a photo matcher

Lakeglass starts with the thing in your hand. Instead of asking for a photograph and returning a confident-looking label, it guides you through observable evidence: shoreline, color, thickness, form, opacity, surface condition, markings, and any diagnostic feature that survived the lake.

The result is a careful field reading rather than a claim of certainty: a likely object type, historical context, occurrence, and the evidence behind the interpretation.

## Read the object in front of you

The identification flow treats color as one clue among many. A pale aqua fragment means something different when it is thin and curved than when it is a heavy base, and raised lettering or a surviving rim can be more useful than color alone. Lake Michigan shoreline context can strengthen or weaken a hypothesis without pretending to prove where an object originated.

## Keep known provenance separate

Where and when a specimen was found are recorded as known provenance. Historical origin, object type, and age remain inferences. Keeping those two categories separate makes the archive more useful over time and leaves room for a reading to change when better evidence appears.

## Research with local context

Lakeglass uses source-backed regional and beach-glass research to explain why a reading was made. The interface surfaces the clues, occurrence rating, local shoreline context, and research basis rather than hiding the reasoning behind a single answer.

## Offline, private, and personal

The app is an installable PWA with a local IndexedDB specimen archive, import/export, and offline support. There are no accounts, no server-side collection database, no photo storage, and no camera identification. The collection stays with the person doing the finding.
